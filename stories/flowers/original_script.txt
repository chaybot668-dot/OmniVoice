# THE WOMAN WHO KEPT SENDING FLOWERS TO MY HOUSE
### HER SIDE OF THE STORY

## OPENING HOOK

The first bouquet arrived on a Friday.

Two dozen white roses.

No card. No name. No explanation.

I thought someone had made a mistake, so I called the florist. They told me the flowers had been paid for in advance and delivered to the correct address.

The following Friday, another bouquet arrived.

This time, pink peonies.

Then came yellow tulips, white lilies, and red roses.

Every week, without fail, flowers appeared on my doorstep.

My husband, Daniel, thought it was funny at first.

“Maybe you have a secret admirer,” he joked.

But after the sixth bouquet, he stopped laughing.

And when I finally discovered who was sending them, I understood why my husband had been so frightened.

The woman sending flowers wasn't trying to steal my husband.

She was trying to tell me what he had spent twelve years making sure I never knew.

And the worst part?

She knew exactly which flowers I liked.

Because she had known me long before I ever met Daniel.

## CHAPTER 1 — THE FIRST BOUQUET

My name is Natalie.

I was thirty-nine years old when the flowers started arriving.

Daniel and I had been married for twelve years. We lived in a comfortable house outside Boston, with a small garden, a white kitchen, and a front porch that I spent far too much time decorating for every season.

From the outside, our marriage looked almost boring.

And I mean that as a compliment.

We had routines. Sunday breakfasts. Grocery shopping on Wednesday evenings. Dinner together whenever Daniel wasn't working late.

He was an architect. Quiet, organized, and the kind of man who remembered to replace the batteries in the smoke detector before they died.

I worked as a freelance interior designer.

We had no children, although we had talked about it more than once. Eventually, we stopped having the conversation. Neither of us admitted that the silence bothered us.

I believed we were happy.

Not the kind of happy people post about online.

The quieter kind.

The kind that comes from knowing someone will be sitting at the kitchen table when you come home.

That was why the first bouquet confused me.

It arrived on a Friday morning while I was making coffee.

The delivery driver left a large box on the porch.

Inside were white roses arranged beautifully in a glass vase.

There was no note.

I checked the box again, thinking perhaps a card had slipped beneath the wrapping.

Nothing.

Daniel came downstairs a few minutes later.

“Those are beautiful,” he said.

“Did you send them?”

He looked at the flowers.

“No. Anniversary?”

“Our anniversary isn't for another four months.”

He shrugged.

“Maybe one of your clients?”

I laughed.

“Clients don't usually send two dozen roses.”

He kissed my forehead and left for work.

I put the flowers on the dining table.

They made the entire room feel different.

For a moment, I enjoyed the mystery.

Perhaps someone had noticed something about me that I hadn't noticed myself.

But the feeling didn't last.

The following Friday, another delivery arrived.

Pink peonies.

Again, no card.

I called the florist.

The woman on the phone confirmed the delivery was intentional. The order had been placed weeks earlier, and payment had already been processed.

“Can you tell me who sent them?”

“I'm sorry, ma'am. The customer requested that the sender's name remain private.”

“Did they leave a message?”

“No. Just the flowers.”

I thanked her and hung up.

That evening, I told Daniel.

He smiled, but something about his expression seemed delayed.

“Maybe someone is trying to be romantic.”

“It's getting strange.”

“Then enjoy it while it lasts.”

He said it casually.

But I noticed he didn't look at the flowers again.

## CHAPTER 2 — EVERY FRIDAY

By the fourth week, the deliveries had become a ritual.

Every Friday morning, I expected the doorbell.

Sometimes I heard the delivery truck before it stopped outside.

Sometimes I watched from the kitchen window.

The flowers changed every week.

Yellow tulips.

White lilies.

Pink garden roses.

Once, an arrangement of pale blue hydrangeas.

They were always fresh, expensive, and carefully selected.

I started keeping photographs of them.

Not because I was sentimental.

Because I wanted to understand the pattern.

The fifth bouquet arrived with something unusual.

A small piece of folded paper had been tucked between the stems.

It contained only one sentence.

Some things should never have been hidden from you.

I read it three times.

My stomach tightened.

I called Daniel immediately.

He didn't answer.

I sent him a photograph of the note.

His reply came almost twenty minutes later.

Probably some stupid prank. Don't let it bother you.

I stared at the message.

A prank?

Someone was spending hundreds of dollars sending flowers to my house, and Daniel thought it was a prank?

That evening, he arrived home later than usual.

He barely touched dinner.

“Did you find out anything?” he asked.

“About the flowers?”

He nodded.

“No. Why?”

“Just wondering.”

I put down my fork.

“Daniel, do you know something?”

He looked at me.

For a second, I thought he was going to tell me something.

Instead, he smiled.

“Of course not.”

Then he changed the subject.

I tried to let it go.

But the note remained in my mind.

Some things should never have been hidden from you.

I began wondering whether the flowers were connected to an affair.

Perhaps Daniel had another relationship.

Perhaps the sender was a woman who knew about me.

I hated that my mind went there.

But I hated even more that Daniel seemed more interested in dismissing the situation than helping me understand it.

The next Friday, no flowers arrived.

For the first time in six weeks, the porch was empty.

I should have felt relieved.

Instead, I felt as though someone had stopped speaking halfway through a sentence.

## CHAPTER 3 — THE WOMAN AT THE FLORIST

On Saturday morning, I drove to the florist.

I brought photographs of every bouquet.

The owner, an older woman named Margaret, recognized the arrangements immediately.

“You've been receiving those?” she asked.

“Yes. Every Friday.”

She hesitated.

Then she looked toward the back of the shop.

“I'm not sure I'm supposed to discuss the order.”

“Please. I just want to know why someone is sending them.”

Margaret studied my face.

“Have you ever known a woman named Claire?”

The name meant nothing to me.

“No.”

Margaret lowered her voice.

“She asked us to use a particular selection of flowers. She said you would understand eventually.”

“Claire?”

Margaret nodded.

“Is that her full name?”

“I only know the first name.”

I thanked her and left.

On the drive home, I searched through my contacts, old emails, and social media.

Nothing.

I had never known anyone named Claire who might have a reason to send me flowers.

When I got home, Daniel's car was in the driveway.

He was standing in the kitchen.

“Where have you been?”

“The florist.”

His face changed.

“What?”

“They know the sender's first name. Claire.”

The glass in his hand stopped halfway to his mouth.

It was a small reaction.

But I saw it.

“Do you know someone named Claire?”

He put the glass down.

“No.”

The answer came too quickly.

“Are you sure?”

“Natalie, why are you doing this?”

“Because someone is sending flowers to our house and leaving messages suggesting you've hidden something from me.”

He rubbed his forehead.

“It's probably someone trying to cause trouble.”

“Then why are you so upset?”

“I'm not upset.”

We stood in silence.

Then he walked upstairs.

I remained in the kitchen, staring at the empty doorway.

For the first time in twelve years, I wondered whether my husband had become a stranger without me noticing.

## CHAPTER 4 — THE OLD PHOTOGRAPH

That evening, I went looking for our marriage documents.

I wanted something ordinary to distract myself.

Instead, I found an old photograph inside a storage box.

It was taken at a summer garden party.

Daniel was younger, standing beside a woman with dark hair.

They were smiling.

His arm rested around her shoulders.

On the back, someone had written:

Daniel and Claire — August 2011.

I sat on the floor.

August 2011.

Daniel and I met in early 2012.

We married a year later.

The photograph had been taken before I knew him.

I searched the box.

There were more photographs.

Daniel and Claire at a restaurant.

Daniel and Claire on a beach.

Daniel holding a small bouquet of flowers while Claire laughed beside him.

They looked comfortable together.

Not like strangers.

Not like people who had briefly dated.

They looked like a couple who had built a life.

I heard the front door open.

Daniel was home.

I carried the photograph downstairs.

He stopped when he saw it.

“Where did you find that?”

“In the storage box.”

He didn't move.

“Who was she?”

He took a long breath.

“Someone I used to know.”

“Her name is Claire.”

“Yes.”

“Were you together?”

He looked away.

“We were engaged.”

The word seemed to change the temperature of the room.

“Engaged?”

“It was a long time ago.”

“How long?”

“Before you.”

“Why have I never heard of her?”

Daniel sat down.

“It wasn't a serious part of my life anymore.”

I looked at the photographs.

“You were engaged to her, Daniel. How is that not serious?”

He didn't answer.

I waited.

Finally, he said, “We broke up. It was difficult. I didn't want to bring old relationships into our marriage.”

“That's your explanation?”

“I didn't think it mattered.”

I put the photograph on the table.

“Then why does she keep sending flowers to our house?”

Daniel stared at the picture.

And for the first time, he looked genuinely afraid.

## CHAPTER 5 — THE PART HE LEFT OUT

Daniel told me the relationship had ended badly.

He said Claire had wanted a different life.

He said they had argued about money, marriage, and where they would live.

He said they eventually went their separate ways.

I listened.

But something was missing.

There were too many careful pauses.

Too many sentences that ended before they reached the important part.

“Did she leave you?” I asked.

“No.”

“Did you leave her?”

He rubbed his hands together.

“It wasn't that simple.”

I almost laughed.

People always say that when the simple answer is one they don't want to give.

“Did you hurt her?”

He looked at me.

“I made mistakes.”

“What mistakes?”

He stood up.

“Natalie, I don't want to spend our evening discussing someone I haven't spoken to in years.”

I looked at him.

“Then tell me why she is sending flowers.”

He said nothing.

The next morning, I noticed Daniel had taken the old photographs.

All of them.

I searched the house and eventually found them inside his office drawer.

He had placed them in an envelope.

I didn't confront him.

Not yet.

Instead, I called Margaret again.

I asked whether Claire had left any instructions with the florist.

This time, Margaret agreed to check.

She called me back that afternoon.

“She asked us to send white roses first,” she said.

“Why?”

“She said they were your favorite.”

I froze.

“How would she know that?”

There was a pause.

“Because she told us she used to know you.”

I sat down.

“What?”

“She said you were friends.”

Friends.

I searched my memory again.

There had been a Claire.

Not someone I had forgotten completely.

Someone I had tried very hard not to remember.

A girl from my university days.

A girl who disappeared from my life after one terrible summer.

Her full name had been Claire Bennett.

And suddenly, I remembered everything.

## CHAPTER 6 — THE SUMMER I FORGOT

Claire and I met during our second year of college.

We weren't best friends, but we were close.

We shared classes, studied together, and occasionally spent weekends at each other's apartments.

She was funny, confident, and unusually good at making people feel understood.

I remembered her telling me about a man she loved.

His name was Daniel.

At the time, I had never met him.

She showed me photographs.

She talked about their future.

She wanted to get married.

And then, one afternoon, she called me crying.

She told me Daniel had been involved with someone else.

She said she had discovered messages between him and another woman.

She didn't know whether to leave him.

I remembered listening to her.

I remembered telling her she deserved better.

But I also remembered something else.

A few days later, Daniel contacted me.

He said Claire was misunderstanding the situation.

He said she was emotional and had been struggling.

He asked me not to make things worse.

I believed him.

Or perhaps I wanted to believe him.

He told me the relationship was already ending.

He said Claire had become controlling.

He said she was trying to turn their friends against him.

I made a mistake.

I stopped answering Claire's calls.

I told myself I was staying out of their relationship.

Then I heard she had moved away.

We never spoke again.

Years later, I met Daniel at a professional event.

He was charming.

He listened.

He remembered details.

We began dating.

I never connected him to Claire's Daniel.

I didn't know his last name had changed professionally.

I didn't recognize him from the old photographs.

And he never told me.

Now I understood why he had never wanted to discuss his past.

He hadn't simply hidden an old engagement.

He had hidden the fact that he had once been involved with someone I knew.

And he had allowed me to believe that the relationship ended for reasons I never questioned.

## CHAPTER 7 — THE WOMAN WHO SENT THE FLOWERS

The seventh bouquet arrived on a Monday.

Not Friday.

It was a simple arrangement of white lilies.

There was a card this time.

I think it's time we talked.

Below the sentence was an address.

I called the number printed on the florist's receipt.

A woman answered.

“Hello?”

I recognized her voice immediately.

“Claire?”

Silence.

Then she said, “Natalie.”

I gripped the phone.

“Why are you sending flowers?”

“I wanted to speak to you.”

“You could have called.”

“I tried. Years ago.”

I closed my eyes.

“I know.”

“I don't think you do.”

Her voice was calm.

Not angry.

That somehow made it worse.

“Can we meet?” she asked.

I looked toward the staircase.

Daniel was upstairs.

“Yes,” I said.

We agreed to meet at a small café the following afternoon.

I barely slept that night.

Daniel noticed.

He asked what was wrong.

I told him Claire wanted to meet.

He immediately objected.

“You shouldn't go.”

“Why?”

“Because she wants to create problems.”

“Maybe she wants to explain something.”

“There is nothing to explain.”

I studied his face.

“You've said that for twelve years.”

He looked away.

I knew then that I was going.

## CHAPTER 8 — WHAT SHE REMEMBERED

Claire was sitting near the window when I arrived.

She looked older, of course.

Her hair was shorter.

There were faint lines around her eyes.

But I recognized her immediately.

She stood when she saw me.

For a moment, neither of us spoke.

Then she said, “You look well.”

I almost smiled.

“You too.”

We sat down.

She ordered coffee.

I didn't.

I wanted answers.

“Why the flowers?”

Claire looked at her hands.

“Because I didn't know how else to reach you.”

“You could have written a letter.”

“I did.”

I looked at her.

“What?”

“Several years ago. Daniel intercepted it.”

My stomach tightened.

“How do you know?”

“Because he called me.”

She took a breath.

“He told me you were happy. He said I had no right to disturb your life. He also said you knew everything about our past.”

I stared at her.

“He told you I knew?”

“Yes.”

“I didn't.”

Claire nodded slowly.

“I suspected that.”

She reached into her handbag and took out an envelope.

Inside were copies of old emails.

The first was from Daniel to Claire.

It was dated several months before I met him.

He had apologized for the way things ended.

He admitted that he had been seeing another woman while he was still engaged.

He also admitted that he had allowed Claire to believe she was imagining things when she questioned him.

I read the email twice.

Then I looked at Claire.

“Why didn't you tell me when I started dating him?”

“I didn't know you were dating him until much later.”

“Then why didn't you contact me?”

“I tried.”

She showed me another email.

A message she had sent to an old address.

It had been forwarded.

Daniel had replied from an account I recognized.

My husband's email.

He had told her not to contact me.

He had written that I knew about the engagement and had chosen to move forward.

That was a lie.

A deliberate one.

I put the paper down.

“Why are you telling me now?”

Claire looked out the window.

“Because my mother died last year.”

I didn't understand.

“What does that have to do with me?”

“She kept something for me. Something Daniel gave her when we were together.”

Claire reached into her bag again.

This time, she pulled out a small photograph.

It showed Daniel standing in front of a house.

A house I recognized.

Our house.

But the photograph was old.

Taken before Daniel and I bought it.

I looked at her.

“Why do you have this?”

Claire's voice became quieter.

“Because Daniel once promised we would live there.”

## CHAPTER 9 — THE HOUSE THAT WASN'T OURS

I stared at the photograph.

The house in the picture was the same house Daniel and I lived in.

The same front porch.

The same white windows.

The same stone path leading to the front door.

“How is this possible?”

Claire explained.

Years before I met Daniel, he had been working on a renovation project in the neighborhood.

He had shown her the house.

They had talked about buying it one day.

He told her it would be their home.

They had even discussed the rooms.

A study for him.

A studio for her.

A garden where they could grow flowers.

But they never bought it.

Their relationship ended before that happened.

Years later, Daniel and I found the property through a real estate agent.

I had fallen in love with it immediately.

Daniel had agreed to the purchase.

He never mentioned that he had once imagined living there with another woman.

I felt foolish.

Not because the house had once mattered to them.

But because he had allowed me to build a life inside a story he had carefully edited.

“Did he know I would buy this house?” I asked.

Claire shook her head.

“I don't think so. But when I saw the photographs you posted after moving in, I recognized it.”

“Why didn't you contact me then?”

“I was afraid you would think I was trying to ruin your marriage.”

I looked at her.

“Were you?”

“No.”

She held my gaze.

“I don't want Daniel back, Natalie.”

I believed her.

There was no jealousy in her expression.

No desire to reclaim something.

Only exhaustion.

“I sent the flowers because I wanted you to know that the story he told you wasn't the whole story.”

I asked the question I had been avoiding.

“Did he cheat on you?”

Claire nodded.

“Yes.”

“Was it with someone else?”

“Yes.”

“Who?”

She hesitated.

Then she said something that made my hands go cold.

“I never knew her name.”

I looked down.

The woman in the story was no longer a stranger.

She was the person Daniel had married.

Me.

## CHAPTER 10 — THE TRUTH ABOUT OUR BEGINNING

I drove home without turning on the radio.

I kept thinking about the timeline.

Daniel and I had met in early 2012.

Claire's engagement had ended in late 2011.

The dates were close.

Too close.

I remembered our first months together.

Daniel had told me he had recently ended a difficult relationship.

He said he had been single for almost a year.

He said he wanted something honest.

I had believed him.

Now I wondered whether our relationship had started before his previous one had truly ended.

When I arrived home, Daniel was in the kitchen.

He looked at me.

“Where were you?”

“With Claire.”

He closed his eyes.

“I told you not to go.”

“You told me not to ask questions.”

“Natalie—”

“Were you seeing me while you were still engaged to her?”

He didn't answer.

That was enough.

I felt something inside me become very still.

“How long?”

He sat down.

“It wasn't like that.”

“Then tell me what it was like.”

He stared at the table.

“We were having problems. I thought the relationship was over.”

“But you hadn't ended it.”

“I was going to.”

“When?”

He didn't answer.

I stepped closer.

“Did you tell her about me?”

“No.”

“Did you tell me about her?”

“No.”

“So both of us were living inside different versions of the same lie.”

He finally looked up.

“I was afraid of losing you.”

I shook my head.

“You were afraid of being exposed.”

He stood.

“I made a mistake twelve years ago. I can't change that.”

“No. But you could have told me.”

“I wanted a fresh start.”

I looked at him.

“A fresh start built on a lie isn't a fresh start.”

He tried to reach for my hand.

I stepped back.

For the first time in our marriage, I didn't want him to touch me.

## CHAPTER 11 — THE LAST BOUQUET

The following Friday, another bouquet arrived.

This time, it was not addressed to me.

It was addressed to Daniel.

Inside were twelve white roses.

And a card.

One for every year you kept the truth from her.

I stood in the doorway holding the card.

Daniel came downstairs.

When he saw the flowers, his face changed.

He read the message.

Then he sat down.

I didn't ask him to explain.

I was tired of asking questions that he had already decided not to answer.

Instead, I asked something else.

“Is there anything more?”

He looked at me.

“What do you mean?”

“Anything else you've hidden. Anything else I might discover from someone else.”

He remained silent for a long time.

Then he admitted that after the breakup, he had contacted Claire several times.

He had apologized.

He had asked her not to tell anyone about the overlap between their relationship and ours.

He said he was ashamed.

He said he wanted to protect the life he had built.

I listened.

Then I asked the question that mattered most.

“Did you ever think I deserved to know?”

He looked down.

“I thought telling you would destroy everything.”

I nodded.

“And keeping it from me didn't?”

He had no answer.

That evening, I slept in the guest room.

I didn't cry.

I simply lay awake, listening to the rain against the windows.

The house felt different.

Not because the walls had changed.

Because I finally understood that I had never known the entire story of the place I called home.

## CHAPTER 12 — WHAT FLOWERS CAN'T FIX

The next few weeks were difficult.

Daniel wanted to repair our marriage immediately.

He offered explanations.

He apologized.

He suggested counseling.

He even offered to show me every email and message he had kept.

But trust doesn't return because someone finally decides to tell the truth.

It returns slowly, if it returns at all.

I agreed to counseling.

Not because I had decided to stay.

Because I wanted to understand whether the marriage we had built could survive the truth.

Claire and I spoke once more.

I thanked her for telling me.

She apologized for the way she had approached me.

I told her I understood.

Before we ended the call, she said something I still remember.

“I didn't send the flowers because I wanted you to hurt. I sent them because I knew what it felt like to be the only person in a room who didn't know the truth.”

I didn't know what to say.

After we hung up, I looked at the flowers on my dining table.

For months, I had thought they were evidence of another woman trying to enter my marriage.

But they had been something else.

A message from someone who had once been silenced.

A reminder that secrets don't disappear simply because people stop talking about them.

Eventually, Daniel and I made a decision to live separately for a while.

We didn't announce it.

We didn't turn it into a dramatic public story.

We simply admitted that we needed space.

I stayed in the house.

Daniel rented an apartment closer to his office.

The first Friday after he moved out, the doorbell rang.

I opened the door.

There was one final bouquet.

White roses.

No envelope.

No note.

I carried them inside and placed them on the dining table.

Then I called the florist.

I asked them to thank Claire.

And I asked them not to send any more flowers.

I didn't need another reminder.

I had finally heard the message.

## ENDING — THE STORY BEHIND THE FLOWERS

People sometimes ask me whether I regret learning the truth.

For a long time, I thought I would have been happier if the flowers had never arrived.

If the first bouquet had been the last.

If I had never found the photographs.

If I had never sat across from Claire in that café.

But I don't believe that anymore.

The flowers didn't destroy my marriage.

They revealed what was already there.

A relationship built on affection can still contain dishonesty.

A person can be loving in a thousand small ways and still hide something that changes the meaning of everything.

And sometimes, the most painful betrayal isn't what happened before you met someone.

It's discovering that they made a choice to keep you from knowing the truth.

I still keep flowers in the house.

Not every week.

Not because someone is sending them.

Sometimes I buy them myself.

White roses, mostly.

They remind me that beauty and pain can exist in the same place.

And that knowing the truth doesn't always give you the ending you wanted.

But it does give you something more important.

The chance to decide what happens next.

Because for twelve years, I thought I was living in a perfect marriage.

Now I know that a perfect-looking story can still have missing pages.

And I will never again let someone else decide which parts of my life I am allowed to read.

This was my side of the story.

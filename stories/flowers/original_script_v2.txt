# THE WOMAN WHO KEPT SENDING FLOWERS TO MY HOUSE
### HER SIDE OF THE STORY

# OPENING HOOK

The first bouquet arrived on a Friday morning.

Two dozen white roses.

No card. No signature. Just my name printed neatly on a delivery label.

I thought it was a mistake.

Then another bouquet arrived the following Friday.

And another one after that.

For seven weeks, someone sent flowers to my house without leaving a name. At first, my husband Daniel joked that I must have a secret admirer.

But the jokes stopped when the flowers started coming with messages.

The first said:

Some people deserve to know the truth.

The second said:

Ask your husband about the summer of 2011.

When I showed Daniel that message, he didn't laugh.

He didn't even pretend to be confused.

He looked at the card for several seconds, then quietly asked me to throw it away.

That was when I knew the flowers weren't random.

Someone was trying to tell me something about my marriage.

And whoever it was knew exactly how to reach me.

What I didn't know was that the woman sending those flowers had once been my friend.

And the man I had trusted for twelve years had spent just as long making sure I never discovered why she disappeared from my life.

# CHAPTER 1 — THE FIRST DELIVERY

My name is Natalie Bennett. I was thirty-nine years old when the flowers started arriving.

My husband, Daniel, and I had been married for twelve years. We lived in a quiet neighborhood outside Boston, in a two-story house with a small garden, dark wooden floors, and a front porch that I decorated every autumn.

Daniel was an architect. He was practical, organized, and not particularly romantic. He remembered birthdays, paid the bills on time, and checked the locks before going to bed.

I was a freelance interior designer. I worked mostly from home, which meant I spent more time than I cared to admit choosing paint colors, arranging furniture, and talking to clients who couldn't decide between two nearly identical shades of beige.

Our life was comfortable.

We had routines that rarely changed. Sunday breakfast at the kitchen table. Grocery shopping on Wednesday evenings. Dinner together whenever Daniel wasn't working late.

We didn't have children. We had discussed it several times during the early years of our marriage, but neither of us seemed ready to make the decision. Eventually, the subject became less frequent, until it almost disappeared.

I thought we were happy.

Not perfect, but happy enough that I rarely questioned it.

The first bouquet arrived on a Friday morning in late September.

I was making coffee when the doorbell rang. A delivery driver left a large white box on the porch and hurried back to his truck.

Inside were two dozen white roses arranged in a tall glass vase.

They were beautiful.

The petals were almost perfectly open, and the stems were wrapped in a pale ribbon. I checked the box for a card, but there was nothing.

When Daniel came downstairs, he found me photographing the arrangement.

"Someone's feeling generous," he said.

"Did you send them?"

He shook his head.

"Should I have?"

"Maybe. Our anniversary is coming up."

"Our anniversary is in four months."

I laughed.

He kissed my forehead and poured himself some coffee.

"Well, whoever sent them has excellent taste."

I placed the roses on the dining table.

For the rest of the morning, I kept looking at them. It felt nice to receive something unexpected.

The next Friday, another delivery arrived.

Pink peonies.

Again, there was no card.

I called the florist, assuming someone had entered the wrong address.

The woman who answered checked the order.

"Yes, Mrs. Bennett. This delivery is definitely intended for you."

"Can you tell me who sent it?"

"I'm sorry. The customer requested privacy."

I thanked her and hung up.

When I told Daniel, he smiled.

"Maybe you have a secret admirer."

"Maybe I should start charging them rent. The flowers are taking over the dining room."

He laughed.

It seemed harmless.

But that evening, I noticed Daniel had moved the peonies from the dining table to the back porch.

When I asked why, he said they were taking up too much space.

I didn't think much of it.

Not then.

# CHAPTER 2 — THE FRIDAY ROUTINE

By the fourth week, the deliveries had become predictable.

Every Friday morning, I expected the doorbell.

The flowers changed each time.

Yellow tulips.

White lilies.

Pink garden roses.

Pale blue hydrangeas.

They were always fresh, carefully arranged, and more expensive than anything I would normally buy for myself.

I began photographing them.

Partly because I liked the arrangements, but mostly because I wanted to understand who was sending them.

Then, during the fifth week, something changed.

The bouquet was made of white lilies.

A small envelope had been tucked between the stems.

Inside was a card with a single sentence.

Some people deserve to know the truth.

I read it twice.

My first thought was Daniel.

My second was that someone was playing a cruel joke.

I called the florist again.

The woman remembered me.

"I'm sorry this is happening," she said. "The customer arranged several deliveries in advance."

"Can you tell me when the order was placed?"

"About six weeks ago."

"So before the first bouquet arrived?"

"Yes."

That detail stayed with me.

This wasn't someone reacting emotionally to something that had happened yesterday. The deliveries had been planned.

Someone had decided in advance that I needed to receive flowers for several weeks.

That evening, I showed Daniel the card.

He read it.

His expression barely changed.

"That's strange."

"Do you think someone knows us?"

"Probably someone trying to get a reaction."

"From me?"

"From both of us, apparently."

I watched him carefully.

"Does the message mean anything to you?"

He put the card on the counter.

"No."

His answer was calm.

But he didn't look at me.

A few minutes later, I noticed the card was gone.

I asked where it was.

"I threw it away."

"Why?"

"Because it's rubbish, Natalie. Don't let some anonymous person ruin your evening."

I didn't argue.

But the next morning, I searched the kitchen trash.

The card was gone.

So was the envelope.

I stood there feeling foolish.

I had been married to Daniel for twelve years. I had never searched through our garbage looking for something he had thrown away.

I told myself I was overreacting.

Then I found the next message.

It arrived the following Friday.

Ask your husband about the summer of 2011.

I read it once.

Then I read it again.

Daniel was in the shower when the delivery arrived. I placed the card on the kitchen counter and waited.

When he came downstairs, I handed it to him.

"Do you know what this means?"

He looked at the message.

His expression changed.

It wasn't dramatic. He didn't drop the card or turn pale.

But his jaw tightened.

He read it a second time.

Then he placed it on the counter.

"Throw it away."

I stared at him.

"Why?"

"Because this is getting ridiculous."

"Daniel, it's asking about a specific summer."

He picked up his keys.

"I don't know what someone wants from us, but I'm not going to spend my day playing detective."

He left.

I remained in the kitchen.

The summer of 2011.

I didn't know why those words mattered.

But I knew they mattered to him.

# CHAPTER 3 — THE NAME AT THE FLOWER SHOP

The following Saturday, I drove to the florist.

The shop was small and old-fashioned, with wooden shelves, handwritten price tags, and buckets of flowers arranged near the front window.

An older woman named Margaret was working behind the counter.

I showed her photographs of the arrangements.

She recognized them immediately.

"You're the woman who's been receiving these," she said.

"Yes. I need to know who is sending them."

Margaret hesitated.

"I'm not sure I'm supposed to discuss customer information."

"I understand. But someone is sending me messages about my husband. I need to know whether there's a reason."

She looked at me for a moment.

Then she asked a question I wasn't expecting.

"Have you ever known a woman named Claire?"

The name stopped me.

Claire.

I hadn't heard that name in years.

"Claire what?"

"She didn't give me a last name. She placed the orders in person."

"Can you describe her?"

Margaret thought for a moment.

"Dark hair. Around your age, perhaps a little older. Very polite. She seemed to know exactly what she wanted."

"Did she say why she was sending flowers?"

"She said you would understand eventually."

I felt a chill move through me.

"Did she leave a phone number?"

Margaret checked the records.

The order had been paid in advance, but the customer had requested that her details remain private.

I left without an answer.

On the drive home, I tried to remember every Claire I had ever known.

There were several.

But only one name made me feel uncomfortable.

Claire Morgan.

We had been friends at university.

Not best friends, but close enough to share classes, meals, and personal problems.

We had lost contact after graduation.

I hadn't spoken to her in years.

I wondered whether the woman sending flowers could be the same Claire.

But why would she contact me now?

And why would she mention Daniel?

When I arrived home, my husband was in the kitchen.

I put my bag down.

"The florist told me the sender's name."

Daniel looked up.

"Who?"

"Claire."

His hand stopped moving.

Only for a moment.

Then he continued pouring water into his glass.

"I don't know anyone named Claire."

I studied him.

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

"Because you look like you recognize the name."

He placed the glass on the counter.

"Natalie, this is becoming an obsession."

"Someone is sending flowers to our house and leaving messages about you."

"And I've told you I don't know who it is."

"Then why are you so defensive?"

He stared at me.

For a moment, neither of us spoke.

Then he picked up his phone and walked into his office.

I stood in the kitchen, thinking about the way he had reacted.

He hadn't asked which Claire.

He hadn't asked how I knew her.

He had simply denied knowing anyone with that name.

And somehow, that answer made me more suspicious than any confession could have.

# CHAPTER 4 — A FACE FROM THE PAST

That evening, I opened an old university photo album.

I hadn't looked through it in years.

Most of the pictures were ordinary. Graduation parties, crowded apartments, birthday dinners, and friends standing outside lecture halls.

Then I found a photograph of Claire.

She was standing beside me at a campus garden party.

Her dark hair was tied back, and she was laughing at something outside the frame.

I remembered her clearly now.

Claire had been warm, funny, and direct. She was the sort of person who could make a difficult conversation feel manageable.

We used to talk about relationships.

She had once told me she was seeing a man named Daniel.

I remembered the name because she spoke about him often.

She showed me a few photographs, but I never met him.

At the time, I was busy with my own studies and a part-time job. I had no reason to remember the face of someone I had only seen in a photograph.

Then came the summer of 2011.

Claire called me one evening.

She was crying.

She told me she had discovered messages between Daniel and another woman.

She believed he had been unfaithful.

I remembered listening to her for almost an hour.

She kept asking whether she was being unreasonable.

Whether she was imagining things.

Whether she should confront him.

I told her she deserved honesty.

A few days later, Daniel contacted me.

I was surprised.

He said Claire had been going through a difficult period. He claimed she had misunderstood the messages and that their relationship was already falling apart.

He asked me not to get involved.

I didn't know what to believe.

I was young, and I didn't want to become part of someone else's relationship.

So I stopped answering Claire's calls.

It was easier than taking a side.

Eventually, she moved away.

We lost contact.

I had never learned what happened between them.

I had never learned Daniel's last name.

And I had never connected the man Claire had spoken about to the man I eventually married.

Until now.

I searched the album for more photographs.

There was one picture of Claire holding a small bouquet of white roses.

On the back, she had written:

For the people who remember.

I stared at the handwriting.

It looked familiar.

The next morning, I placed the photograph beside the anonymous card.

The letters were similar.

The same narrow handwriting.

The same way the letter "r" leaned slightly to the right.

I didn't need a handwriting expert to understand what I was looking at.

The woman sending flowers was Claire.

And she had been trying to reach me for a reason.

# CHAPTER 5 — THE STORY DANIEL TOLD ME

I waited until Daniel came home.

The photograph was on the dining table.

Beside it was the card about the summer of 2011.

He stopped when he saw them.

"Where did you get that?"

"An old album."

He looked at Claire's photograph.

"Do you remember her?"

I nodded.

"She was my friend."

Daniel pulled out a chair.

For a moment, he said nothing.

Then he admitted they had been together.

They had dated for several years.

They had become engaged.

They had planned to marry in the spring of 2012.

I looked at him.

"You were engaged to my friend?"

"Yes."

"And you never told me?"

"I didn't think it was relevant."

"Daniel, I knew her."

"I know."

That answer made my stomach tighten.

"You knew we were friends?"

He nodded.

"When did you know?"

"When you mentioned her name after we started dating."

I stared at him.

"You knew who I was before you married me?"

He shook his head.

"I didn't know at first. I recognized your name later."

"And you said nothing?"

"I didn't know how to explain it."

I leaned back.

"Try."

He looked at the photograph.

He told me that his relationship with Claire had ended before he and I started dating.

He said they had become unhappy.

They argued frequently.

He wanted to focus on his career, while Claire wanted to settle down.

He said the relationship had become exhausting.

I listened.

Then I asked the question I had been avoiding.

"Did you cheat on her?"

He looked at me.

"No."

I didn't believe him.

"Then why did she tell me she found messages between you and another woman?"

Daniel rubbed his forehead.

"She misunderstood something."

"What did she misunderstand?"

"I was talking to someone from work."

"About what?"

"Nothing important."

"Then why did you hide it?"

He stood up.

"Natalie, this happened more than twelve years ago."

"That doesn't make it irrelevant."

"I made mistakes. We all did."

I looked at him.

"Did you tell Claire the truth?"

He didn't answer.

I realized then that I was still hearing the same explanation he had given me years earlier.

Claire was emotional.

She misunderstood.

The relationship was already over.

The details didn't matter.

Daniel had used the same language to silence both of us.

And it had worked.

Until someone started sending flowers.

# CHAPTER 6 — THE EMAILS

The following Monday, I received an email from an unfamiliar address.

The subject line was simple.

For Natalie.

There was no greeting.

Just a short message.

I know this is difficult. I don't want to hurt you. But I think you should see what happened before you decide whether Daniel's version is the truth.

An attachment followed.

It contained several printed emails from 2011.

The first was a message from Claire to Daniel.

She asked him directly whether he was seeing someone else.

She wrote that she had seen messages on his phone and wanted him to tell her the truth.

Daniel's reply was short.

He said she was imagining things.

He said the woman was a colleague.

He accused Claire of being suspicious and controlling.

The next email was dated four days later.

Claire wrote that she had spoken to someone who confirmed Daniel had been spending time with the other woman outside work.

She asked him to stop lying.

Daniel replied that their relationship was already over and that she should stop trying to control his life.

But the date mattered.

Their engagement was still active.

The wedding invitation was still being prepared.

Claire had not been told that the relationship was over.

She had been told they were working through their problems.

I read the emails several times.

Then I reached the final attachment.

It was a message Daniel had sent to Claire after she moved away.

He apologized for how things had ended.

He admitted he had been seeing another woman.

He said he should have told her the truth earlier.

He also asked her not to contact his friends or family.

I closed the laptop.

For several minutes, I sat in silence.

The woman Claire had suspected was real.

The messages were real.

The affair was real.

And Daniel had spent years telling people that Claire had misunderstood.

I called him.

He answered after the third ring.

"Did you send Claire those emails?"

There was a long pause.

"Where did you get them?"

"That isn't an answer."

"Natalie, we need to talk about this in person."

"Did you send them?"

"Yes."

I closed my eyes.

"Why did you tell me she was imagining things?"

He sighed.

"Because I didn't want you to think badly of me."

I almost laughed.

"You were more concerned about what I thought of you than what she went through."

"That isn't fair."

"Then explain it."

He said he had been ashamed.

He had been involved with a woman from his workplace. The relationship had started as friendship, then developed into something physical.

He said it lasted several months.

He eventually ended it.

He and Claire separated shortly afterward.

He admitted that he had lied to Claire about the affair.

He admitted that he had lied to me about the reason their engagement ended.

But he insisted that the affair had ended before we met.

I asked him one final question.

"Did you ever think I deserved to know that you had cheated on someone who was my friend?"

He said nothing.

And I understood that his silence was not an accident.

It was the answer he had been avoiding for twelve years.

# CHAPTER 7 — THE WOMAN WHO KNEW MY NAME

I agreed to meet Claire.

We chose a small café near the river.

She was already sitting by the window when I arrived.

Her hair was shorter than I remembered. She wore a dark blue coat and had a tired expression.

For a moment, neither of us spoke.

Then she stood.

"Natalie."

I hugged her.

It felt strange.

We had once been friends, then strangers, and now we were sitting across from each other because we had married the same man at different points in our lives.

I ordered coffee.

She already had hers.

"Why the flowers?" I asked.

Claire looked down.

"I tried calling you years ago."

"I know."

"I sent an email too."

"I never received it."

"Daniel told me you knew everything."

I shook my head.

"I didn't know anything."

Claire nodded.

"That's what I thought."

She explained that she had discovered Daniel's affair in the summer of 2011.

She had found messages between him and a woman from his office.

At first, he denied everything.

Then he accused her of being jealous.

He told her that she was imagining things.

When she finally found enough evidence, he admitted the affair.

But by then, she said, the damage had already been done.

"What happened after that?" I asked.

"He said he wanted to repair the relationship. He promised he would stop seeing her. I believed him for a while."

"Did he?"

Claire shook her head.

"Not immediately."

She told me that Daniel had continued contacting the other woman for several weeks.

The relationship eventually ended, but the trust was gone.

Claire cancelled the wedding.

She moved away.

And she stopped trying to contact him.

I asked why she hadn't told me everything back then.

"I tried," she said. "But Daniel contacted you first."

I remembered that phone call.

His careful explanation.

His request that I stay out of it.

Claire looked at me.

"I knew you didn't want to be caught between us. I didn't blame you for that."

I felt ashamed.

"I should have listened."

"Maybe. But you were young. And he was very convincing."

She took a small envelope from her handbag.

Inside was a printed photograph of Daniel standing beside the woman from his office.

I didn't recognize her.

"Who is she?"

"I never learned her full name."

Claire explained that the woman had eventually left the company. She had no way of contacting her, and she had never tried to find her afterward.

"Why did you send the flowers now?" I asked.

Claire looked at me for a long moment.

"Because I saw a photograph of you and Daniel online."

She explained that she had discovered we were married several years earlier.

She had recognized my name.

She had considered contacting me, but she was afraid I would assume she wanted Daniel back.

Then, the previous year, her mother had become seriously ill.

While sorting through her mother's belongings, Claire found an old folder containing copies of the emails from 2011.

Her mother had kept them because she had been the person who helped Claire move away after the engagement ended.

Claire said seeing the emails again made her realize how much of the story had been changed over time.

She didn't want revenge.

She wanted me to know the truth.

"Why the flowers?" I asked again.

Claire smiled sadly.

"Because I remembered you loved white roses."

I looked at her.

"You remembered that?"

"You used to buy them after exams. You said they made your apartment feel peaceful."

I had forgotten that.

Claire hadn't.

She continued.

"I thought if I sent something familiar, you might recognize that it was coming from someone who knew you. I didn't want to frighten you."

I looked down at my coffee.

For months, I had imagined a stranger trying to destroy my marriage.

But the woman sending the flowers had once known me.

She had remembered a small detail from a life I had almost forgotten.

And she had been carrying the truth for twelve years.

# CHAPTER 8 — THE QUESTION DANIEL COULDN'T ANSWER

When I returned home, Daniel was waiting in the living room.

He knew I had met Claire.

He didn't ask how it went.

He asked what she had told me.

I put my coat down.

"She showed me the emails."

He looked away.

"I assumed she would."

"She also told me she tried contacting me."

"I know."

"How?"

Daniel remained silent.

Then he admitted that he had received a message from Claire several years earlier.

She had asked him to let me know the truth.

He had replied that our marriage was strong and that bringing up the past would serve no purpose.

He told her not to contact me again.

I looked at him.

"You made that decision for me?"

"I was trying to protect our marriage."

"No. You were trying to protect yourself."

He shook his head.

"I made a mistake before we met."

"You made a choice after we met. You kept the truth from me every year we were together."

He sat down.

"I didn't want you to think I was a bad person."

I looked at him.

"Daniel, I don't need you to have a perfect past. I needed you to be honest about it."

He said he loved me.

He said our marriage was real.

He said the relationship with Claire had nothing to do with how he felt about me.

I believed that he might love me.

But that was no longer the question.

The question was whether I could trust someone who had repeatedly chosen his own comfort over my right to know the truth.

I asked him something simple.

"If Claire had never sent those flowers, would you ever have told me?"

He looked at me.

His mouth opened.

Then he closed it.

I waited.

He didn't answer.

And in that silence, I understood more than I had learned from all the emails.

He had no plan to tell me.

Not after one year.

Not after five.

Not after twelve.

He had been willing to let me live with a false version of our marriage indefinitely.

# CHAPTER 9 — THE LAST DELIVERY

The following Friday, another bouquet arrived.

This time, it was addressed to Daniel.

Twelve white roses.

A small card was attached.

One for every year you kept the truth from her.

I stood in the doorway holding the card.

Daniel came downstairs.

When he saw the flowers, he stopped.

He read the message.

Then he sat at the dining table.

I didn't ask him to explain.

I was tired of asking questions he had already decided not to answer.

Instead, I asked something else.

"Is there anything more?"

He looked up.

"What do you mean?"

"Anything else you've hidden. Anything else I might discover from someone else."

He remained silent.

Then he told me that after the breakup, he had contacted Claire several times.

He had apologized.

He had asked her not to tell anyone about the affair.

He said he was ashamed.

He said he wanted to start over.

I asked whether he had ever considered telling me that he had been unfaithful in a previous relationship.

He said he hadn't thought it was necessary.

I looked at the flowers.

"Do you understand what this means to me?"

He nodded.

But I wasn't sure he did.

I told him I needed space.

He asked whether I was leaving.

I said I didn't know.

For the first time in twelve years, I slept in the guest room.

I didn't cry.

I simply lay awake, listening to the rain against the windows.

The house felt unfamiliar.

The same furniture.

The same photographs.

The same wedding picture above the fireplace.

But the story behind those photographs had changed.

I began to wonder how many conversations I had remembered incorrectly.

How many times Daniel had avoided a question.

How many moments of silence I had mistaken for peace.

The next morning, I removed our wedding photograph from the living room.

Not because I wanted to erase our marriage.

Because I couldn't look at it without wondering what else I didn't know.

# CHAPTER 10 — WHAT THE FLOWERS COULDN'T FIX

The next few weeks were difficult.

Daniel wanted to repair our marriage immediately.

He apologized.

He suggested counseling.

He offered to show me every email and message he had kept.

But trust doesn't return because someone finally decides to tell the truth.

It returns slowly, if it returns at all.

I agreed to counseling.

Not because I had decided to stay.

Because I wanted to understand whether the marriage we had built could survive the truth.

During one session, our counselor asked Daniel why he had kept the secret for so long.

He said he was afraid of losing me.

The counselor asked him what he thought I had lost.

Daniel didn't answer.

I did.

"I lost the ability to decide whether I wanted this relationship with all the information."

Daniel looked at me.

For the first time, I think he understood that the problem wasn't simply what he had done before our marriage.

It was the choice he had made every day afterward.

Every anniversary.

Every holiday.

Every time I asked him about his past.

Every time Claire's name came up in some unrelated conversation.

He had been given opportunities to tell me.

And every time, he chose silence.

I also had to face my own part in the past.

I had stopped answering Claire's calls.

I had believed Daniel because it was easier than questioning someone I barely knew.

I had told myself I was staying out of their relationship.

But I had abandoned a friend who was asking for help.

That didn't make Daniel's choices my fault.

But it reminded me that silence can hurt people even when we think we're avoiding conflict.

Claire and I spoke once more.

I thanked her for telling me.

She apologized for the way she had approached me.

I told her I understood.

Before we ended the call, she said something I still remember.

"I didn't send the flowers because I wanted you to hurt. I sent them because I knew what it felt like to be the only person in a room who didn't know the truth."

I didn't know what to say.

After we hung up, I looked at the flowers on my dining table.

For months, I had thought they were evidence of another woman trying to enter my marriage.

But they had been something else.

A message from someone who had once been silenced.

A reminder that secrets don't disappear simply because people stop talking about them.

Eventually, Daniel and I decided to live separately for a while.

We didn't announce it.

We didn't turn it into a dramatic public story.

We simply admitted that we needed space.

I stayed in the house.

Daniel rented an apartment closer to his office.

The first Friday after he moved out, the doorbell rang.

I opened the door.

There was one final bouquet.

White roses.

No envelope.

No note.

I carried them inside and placed them on the dining table.

Then I called the florist.

I asked them to thank Claire.

And I asked them not to send any more flowers.

I didn't need another reminder.

I had finally heard the message.

# ENDING — THE STORY BEHIND THE FLOWERS

People sometimes ask me whether I regret learning the truth.

For a long time, I thought I would have been happier if the flowers had never arrived.

If the first bouquet had been the last.

If I had never found the photographs.

If I had never sat across from Claire in that café.

But I don't believe that anymore.

The flowers didn't destroy my marriage.

They revealed what was already there.

A relationship built on affection can still contain dishonesty.

A person can be loving in a thousand small ways and still hide something that changes the meaning of everything.

And sometimes, the most painful betrayal isn't what happened before you met someone.

It's discovering that they made a choice to keep you from knowing the truth.

I still keep flowers in the house.

Not every week.

Not because someone is sending them.

Sometimes I buy them myself.

White roses, mostly.

They remind me that beauty and pain can exist in the same place.

And that knowing the truth doesn't always give you the ending you wanted.

But it does give you something more important.

The chance to decide what happens next.

Because for twelve years, I thought I was living in a perfect marriage.

Now I know that a perfect-looking story can still have missing pages.

And I will never again let someone else decide which parts of my life I am allowed to read.

This was my side of the story.
